### Opening arguments

**Bull Agent**
1. Revenue surged 94% year over year to $1.12 billion alongside a record order book of $3.4 billion, supported by a five-year deployment agreement with a major European logistics group. [VER-06] [VER-07]
2. Shares trade at an attractive 3.1 times forward revenue discount compared to the 4.5 times average of high-growth infrastructure peers, with seven of ten analysts rating the stock Buy and a median price target 38% higher than the current price. [VER-01] [VER-02]
3. Gross margin expanded strongly to 31% from 22% due to falling hardware costs and software subscriptions growing to account for 28% of revenue, leading management to raise full-year revenue guidance twice. [VER-08] [VER-09]
4. The company possesses proprietary fast-charging technology protected by 23 granted patents that cuts charging time by 40%. [VER-04]
5. The founder and CEO retains a 24% ownership stake with zero shares sold since listing, while governance is strengthened by the addition of two independent infrastructure finance directors. [VER-13] [VER-14]

**Bear Agent**
1. The company faces severe customer concentration risk as its largest customer, a European logistics group, accounts for 56% of revenue, up from 41% a year earlier. [VER-12]
2. Net debt to EBITDA rose to 3.2x at quarter end from 2.4x a year earlier due to borrowings for network expansion. [VER-10]
3. Pricing pressure is beginning to emerge in core markets following the announcement of rival charging networks by two utility-backed competitors. [VER-05]
4. An analyst downgraded the stock to Hold in October specifically because of this concentration in a single customer. [VER-03]

### Rebuttal round

**Bull Agent** rebuts the bear's claim #1: While customer concentration has increased, this relationship is anchored by a record $3.4 billion order book backed by a five-year deployment agreement [VER-07], which underpins phenomenal 94% year-over-year revenue growth to $1.12 billion [VER-06].

**Bear Agent** rebuts the bull's claim #1: While revenue and the order book appear impressive, this growth carries extreme customer concentration risk, as the single European logistics group now accounts for 56% of total revenue, up from 41% a year earlier.
